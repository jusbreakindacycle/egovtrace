DO NOT BUILD INFRASTRUCTURE WITHOUT MAINTENANCE.

DO NOT APPROVE PROJECTS WITHOUT MEASURABLE OUTCOMES.

DO NOT LET GOVERNMENT DATA LIVE IN SILOS.

DO NOT TREAT NATURE AS EMPTY SPACE.

And perhaps the most important:

THE PHILIPPINES IS NOT ONE CITY WITH A LOT OF ISLANDS.

It is an archipelago of interconnected ecosystems, cities, rural communities, transport corridors, watersheds and economic systems.

Therefore the master plan must also be an archipelago-scale graph.

The legal system is already much more supportive of this philosophy than it may initially appear: local autonomy under RA 7160, disaster mainstreaming under RA 10121, climate-risk integration under RA 9729, water-quality management under RA 9275, ecological waste management under RA 9003, protected areas under RA 11038, wildlife protection under RA 9147, environmental impact review under PD 1586, public-transport modernization, public-service regulation, procurement reform under RA 12009, PPP under RA 11966, and integrated urban/housing planning under RA 11201 all provide pieces of the architecture. 

The National Adaptation Plan itself makes essentially the same warning from a climate perspective: transport, energy and communications need comprehensive cross-system planning, with substantial projected exposure of transport infrastructure to climate hazards. 

Bottom line

I would not try to “solve flooding.”

I would redesign how the Philippines manages water.

I would not try to “solve traffic.”

I would redesign how Filipinos move people and goods.

I would not simply “build more infrastructure.”

I would build a national physical operating system in which:

water + land + ecology + settlement + transit + roads + ports + airports + energy + communications + economy + government money

are continuously modeled together.

And public transportation would be the mobility priority, while natural flood retention and ecosystem protection would be the first line of flood defense.

That is the kind of plan I would put before Congress as a new Philippine National Water, Mobility and Spatial Resilience Master Plan 2027–2055, rather than another collection of disconnected DPWH and DOTr projects.
