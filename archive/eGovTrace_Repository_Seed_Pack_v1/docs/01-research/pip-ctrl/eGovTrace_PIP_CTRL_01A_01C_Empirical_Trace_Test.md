# eGovTrace PIP-CTRL-01A–01C
## Planning / Investment / Project-Monitoring Landscape Enumeration + Initial Trace Test

**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Status:** EMPIRICAL RESEARCH — preliminary gate result  
**Scope:** National + regional + provincial + city/municipal + barangay planning/investment/project-monitoring surfaces  
**Decision:** Do not modify G0 yet.

---

## 1. Objective

Test whether real Philippine government projects can be traced across:

**PLAN → INVESTMENT PROGRAM → BUDGET → PROCUREMENT → CONTRACT → IMPLEMENTATION**

using official/publicly accessible government records.

The experiment is not yet testing corruption.

It tests **administrative continuity and reconciliation**.

---

## 2. PIP-CTRL-01A — Planning Instrument Enumeration

### National

**Long-Term Development Framework (LTDF)**
- 25-year national planning horizon under RA 12145.
- Intended to provide long-term direction for national development and infrastructure planning.

**Philippine Development Plan (PDP)**
- Current medium-term national development plan.
- Supported by Results Matrices with targets, indicators, means of verification and responsible agencies.

**Sectoral Infrastructure Master Plans**
- RA 12145 requires agency sectoral infrastructure master plans responsive to the LTDF.

**Comprehensive Infrastructure Development Master Plan (CIDMP)**
- Sectoral infrastructure master plans are to be consolidated/vetted into the CIDMP.
- Intended to provide a foundation for forward planning, prioritization and infrastructure investment/program development.

**Regional Development Plans (RDPs)**
- Translate national development priorities to regional level.

**Regional Development Investment Program (RDIP)**
- Regional investment programming layer.
- As of August 2026, DEPDev's PIPOL environment is piloting an RDIP module connected to annual investment programming.

### National sector/program planning

Relevant examples include:
- Integrated Water Resources and Flood Management Master Plan (IWRFMP) requirement for DPWH.
- Transport-sector master plans.
- Water-supply and sanitation planning.
- Irrigation/agriculture-sector plans.
- Other agency-specific infrastructure/sector master plans.

### Local

**Provincial Development and Physical Framework Plan (PDPFP/PPFP)**

**Comprehensive Development Plan (CDP)**

**Comprehensive Land Use Plan (CLUP)**

**Local Development Investment Program (LDIP)**

**Annual Investment Program (AIP)**

**Supplemental AIP / Supplemental LDIP**

**Local Budget / Appropriation Ordinance**

**Barangay Development Plan / Barangay AIP / Barangay Budget**

### Key finding

The Philippine planning architecture is not a single linear master plan.

It is a hierarchy with multiple parallel sectoral and territorial plans.

The research question is therefore whether a project remains identifiable and traceable while moving through those layers.

## 3. PIP-CTRL-01B — Investment / Budget Registry Enumeration

### National investment

**PIPOL**
- Official Public Investment Program online system.
- Contains the rolling list of priority PAPs for national government, GOCCs, GFIs and other national offices/instrumentalities.
- Supports agency PAP submission/update, DEPDev validation and report generation.

**TRIP**
- Three-Year Rolling Infrastructure Program.
- DEPDev/DBM policy framework used to prioritize infrastructure projects for budget preparation.
- The approved TRIP is used in agency infrastructure project review/prioritization during technical budget hearings.

**RDIP module**
- 2026 pilot within PIPOL for regional development investment programming.

### National budget

**NEP**
- Proposal-stage annual budget representation.

**GAA**
- Enacted annual appropriation.
- FY2026 DBM GAA publishes Program/Project/Activity appropriations and DPWH's detailed project tables.

**Agency Annual Procurement Plan (APP)**
- Links planned procurement activities to projects/funds and provides procurement timing.

### Local investment/budget

**LDIP**
- Multi-year local investment programming.

**AIP**
- Annual investment program, representing the year's prioritized PPAs.

**Local Budget**
- Fiscal authorization for local PPAs.

**20% Development Fund**
- Itemized development projects are required; DBM guidance ties the allocation to projects embodied in local development plans.

**Supplemental AIP / Supplemental Budget**
- Important for detecting projects introduced/reprogrammed after the original planning cycle.

### Key finding

There is already a substantial investment-control architecture.

The unknown is not whether an investment registry exists.

The unknown is whether the same project can be joined reliably across:
**PIP/PIPOL → TRIP → NEP → GAA → APP → procurement → contract.**

## 4. PIP-CTRL-01C — Project Monitoring / Disclosure Enumeration

### National project monitoring

**DPWH Infrastructure Transparency Portal**
- Public contract/project surface covering 2016–2026.
- Searchable by contract name, location and contractor.
- Shows procurement/ongoing/completed/terminated status and fields such as cost, accomplishment and completion date.
- The portal presently reports more than 258,000 contracts and approximately ₱6.4T total investment.

**DPWH Procurement / Project Monitoring Reports**
- Agency procurement monitoring reports provide contract IDs, procurement stages, source of funds, amounts and implementation milestones.

**DPWH Annual Infrastructure Program**
- Official project component IDs, UACS/subprogram, physical target, amount, type of work and implementing office.

**FMR Watch**
- DA public project-monitoring portal for FMR projects.
- DA states it integrates official records, budget data, construction milestones, geotagged photos and citizen feedback.

### Procurement

**PhilGEPS**
- Public bid notices provide solicitation/reference numbers, contract IDs, descriptions, ABC, funding source, schedule, procurement mode and status.

**DPWH APP / award / notice-to-proceed / post-award records**
- Useful for joining procurement and implementation records.

### Local project monitoring / disclosure

**DILG Full Disclosure Policy (FDP) Portal**
- LGUs are required to disclose financial information and ongoing/completed government contracts/projects.
- Covers provinces, cities, municipalities and barangays.

**Barangay Full Disclosure Policy**
- Covers budget, income/expenditure, 20% NTA utilization, APP, notices of award and other required disclosures.

**DILG project programs**
- Examples include Conditional Matching Grant to Provinces (CMGP) and Performance Challenge Fund (PCF).

**DevLIVE**
- DILG Region X project-monitoring application allowing citizens to monitor and give feedback on selected local projects including CMGP/ADM/AM projects.

### Key finding

The monitoring landscape is materially richer than a single-project dashboard.

The problem is interoperability.

A project may appear in a planning instrument, budget, procurement system and monitoring portal, but those representations may not expose a common stable project key.

# 7. What the First Empirical Tests Show

## Finding 1 — National projects can already be traced several steps

For the Agos River project, the public record supports:

**program → procurement plan → bid notice → award/contract → notice to proceed**

This is already enough to demonstrate that a multi-system lifecycle is observable.

## Finding 2 — The plan-to-project link is weaker than the procurement-to-contract link

The retrieved project records describe the flood-management program and major-river-basin category, but we have not yet demonstrated:

**specific master plan node → specific project**

This is critical.

## Finding 3 — Local planning is observable but harder to connect downstream

Tangalan's municipal resolutions clearly expose:
- LDIP
- AIP
- supplemental investment programming
- 20% Development Fund reprogramming

But the public search did not immediately expose the downstream procurement/contract record for the exact 2026 projects.

This is a genuine reconciliation/access finding, not evidence of wrongdoing.

## Finding 4 — National/local projects may coexist in the same physical system

Tangalan demonstrates the possibility that:
- LGU-funded river protection
- DPWH-funded river protection
- local disaster planning
- national flood-management programs

may operate around the same river/location.

This creates a potentially valuable future **overlap / coordination control** test.

## Finding 5 — Monitoring is institutionally fragmented, but not absent

DPWH, DA, DILG and LGUs already operate monitoring/disclosure surfaces.

eGovTrace's possible role is therefore not to create another monitoring portal.

It is to reconcile the representations.

**PIP-CTRL-01D — NOT YET PASSED**

# 10. Critical Observation

The most important result so far is a shift in the question.

We are no longer asking:

> “Does government have plans?”

It clearly does.

We are asking:

> **Can the State prove that the project receiving money today is the same project that was justified, prioritized and selected in the applicable planning system?**

And then:

> **Can the State prove that the project being procured is the same project that was budgeted?**

And:

> **Can the State prove that the contract being paid is the same physical project that was approved?**

That is a much cleaner empirical definition of the possible **Project Control** pattern.
